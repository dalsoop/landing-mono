01 Problem

Every plan change
means rebuilding the CAD.

After diagnosis, dentists still turn the plan into per-stage 3D models by hand. A treatment longer than a year needs dozens of them.

A model per stage

Each clear tray moves the teeth a small step, and each step needs its own arch model.

Dozens per case

Longer treatment means more stages, and more CAD work.

Rework on every change

A revised plan sends you back into the existing design.

From the field

Site visits and consultations with a board-certified specialist in integrated dentistry confirmed this repetitive CAD burden.

04 Control

Nothing leaves
without your approval.

Diagnosis, prescription and the final choice stay with the dentist.

Drafts only

Every plan is a proposal until you approve it.

Your conditions hold

The agent never loosens them to pass a rule.

Explicit approval

Plans with violations can't be approved or exported.

No patient identifiers

Patient registration rejects identifying details. Chat messages with ID or phone numbers are refused.

05 How we evaluate

No certification yet.
Here is what we test.

Everything below is in the public repository and can be re-run.

Data

Public scans

Sample cases are real upper-arch scans from the open Poseidon3D dataset (CC BY 4.0).

Rules

Geometry checks

Plans are checked for space shortage, collisions, movement per aligner and stage count. A benchmark runs those checks on five synthetic presets.

Agent

19 scripted scenarios

They cover revisions, plans that honestly fail, unsupported requests, and diagnosis questions the agent hands back to the dentist.

Clinical

One dentist, 12 cases

A dentist judged space and approach for 12 sample scans by eye. We treat it as a qualitative reference and report no score.

The automated tests (uv run pytest) and a rule-based plan (cualign plan) run offline. The verification record in the repository lists what passed and what hasn't been checked. Clinical validity is not evaluated.

06 Compatibility

It reads and writes
plain STL files.

Input

Segmented upper-arch scan

One STL per tooth, numbered in the Universal system, plus an optional gum scan.

Output

ZIP of stage files

Per-tooth STL for every stage and, with a gum scan, a printable arch model per stage.

Not yet tested with specific intraoral scanners, CAD software or 3D printers. Automatic segmentation of one-piece scans is not supported.

09 Current scope

A research prototype.
Here is its scope.

  • Upper arch only. Translation, incisor rotation and vertical correction.
  • Not yet modelled. Rotation of canines and back teeth, torque, the lower arch, occlusion.
  • Computed checks. IPR adjusts space only in the calculation, and the collision check doesn't guarantee zero contact for every tooth pair.
  • Runs locally. No hosted demo, no user accounts.
  • For review. Exports are not validated for manufacturing or wear.

10 FAQ

Questions.

Is cuAlign a medical device?

No. It is a research prototype and must not be used for diagnosis or treatment decisions.

Which cases does it not support?

Lower arches, rotation of canines and back teeth, torque, occlusion, and one-piece scans that are not segmented per tooth.

What happens to scan data?

You run cuAlign on your own machine, and scans and plans stay there. The agent conversation goes through the NVIDIA API. There is no login or per-user isolation, so don't upload identifiable patient data.

How much does it cost?

Nothing. cuAlign is a research prototype and isn't sold.

What are the terms?

Use is covered by the End User License Agreement: research and demonstration only, no warranty. Sample scans are CC BY 4.0, and third-party components keep their own terms.

What do I need to run it?

Python 3.12 and uv. Plans compute without a key. The conversation needs an NVIDIA API key.
